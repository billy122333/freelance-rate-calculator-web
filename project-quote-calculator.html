Project Quote Guide

How to use a project quote calculator without sending underpriced work.

A project quote calculator should do more than multiply hours by a rate. It should help you carry revision risk, business costs, and the uncertainty that sits between a clean scope and the real project.

Why freelancers underquote projects

Underquoting usually happens long before the proposal is sent. It starts when a freelancer uses a low hourly rate, ignores non-billable time, or assumes the scope will stay cleaner than it really does.

The problem is rarely the multiplication. The problem is the assumptions underneath it.

What a project quote should include

  • Estimated delivery hours
  • A rate that already supports your monthly target
  • Room for revisions and ambiguity
  • Tax reserve if that money is not really yours yet
  • A clear floor before optional add-ons or rush pricing
Important: If the project is vague, your safest move is usually to raise the buffer, not to hope the estimate stays accurate.

How to build a quote from your hourly rate

  1. Start with a sustainable freelance hourly rate
  2. Estimate the project hours honestly
  3. Add a risk or revision buffer
  4. Set aside a tax reserve if needed
  5. Use the result as your pricing floor

When a fixed quote makes more sense than hourly billing

Fixed quotes are often easier for clients to approve because they reduce uncertainty. They can also protect you if your delivery is efficient. The catch is that your fixed quote still needs to be anchored to a healthy internal rate.

Use the quote as a floor, not a ceiling

Once you know your floor, you can still price higher for complexity, speed, brand value, or strategic impact. What matters is that you stop sending quotes below the level your business can sustain.

Quick takeaway

A better project quote starts with a better rate. Then it adds enough buffer to survive the real version of the project.
