Freelance Pricing Guide

How to calculate a freelance hourly rate that actually supports your month.

A freelance hourly rate calculator is only useful if it starts from the numbers that matter: your target income, realistic billable hours, overhead, and the margin you need for tax and project risk.

What a good freelance hourly rate should cover

The biggest pricing mistake freelancers make is treating an hourly rate like a random market number. Your rate is not just labor. It needs to carry your take-home goal, non-billable time, software, admin overhead, and the uncertainty that comes with project work.

  • Your monthly take-home income target
  • The billable hours you can actually sell each month
  • Monthly business costs like software, gear, rent, and admin
  • A tax reserve you do not want to accidentally spend
  • A revision or risk buffer for under-scoped work
A simple rule: If your freelance hourly rate does not leave room for non-billable work and surprises, it is probably too low.

Why billable hours matter more than calendar hours

Many freelancers overestimate how many hours they can charge each month. Meetings, outreach, revisions, proposals, and bookkeeping all take time. If you divide your income target by a fantasy workload, your rate will look lower than it should.

A better starting point is to assume that only a portion of your working hours are truly billable. That is why this calculator asks for billable hours directly instead of total work hours.

How to turn monthly goals into an hourly floor

Start by adding your monthly take-home goal and monthly business costs together. Then divide that number by your realistic billable hours. That gives you a break-even style floor before tax reserve and risk buffer. Once you layer those back in, you get something much closer to a sustainable freelance rate.

  1. Set your take-home target
  2. Add monthly business costs
  3. Divide by realistic billable hours
  4. Add a tax reserve percentage
  5. Add a revision or uncertainty buffer

When to raise the rate instead of lowering the scope

If you already know a client will bring extra revisions, stakeholder feedback, or poor briefs, it is usually better to raise the buffer than to pretend the project is clean and simple. A strong rate protects your month before the work starts.

Quick takeaway

A sustainable freelance hourly rate is a pricing floor, not a random average. It should be built from your workload reality and your financial target.
